Ground rules for panellists

  • Same questions, same order, for everyone. Read them as written. No hints to friends.
  • Judge what you heard, not who the person is or how well you know them.
  • Take short notes while they answer, so you remember the detail when you agree the result.
  • Stay in role. Friendly and professional. No laughing, no chatting between answers.
  • Keep it confidential. Nothing about anyone's interview leaves the room.
  • Feedback is specific and kind: "Your walkthrough worked because you explained why you chose…", not "it was good".

The script and questions

Opening · Chair

"Hi [name], thanks for coming in. I'm James, and this is [Panellist A] and [Panellist B]. We're interviewing for the Junior Game Developer trainee role at Anchorlight Games. This will take about ten minutes. We'll ask you four or five questions, and then there'll be time for any questions you have for us. Take your time with your answers. Ready?"

Question 1 · Chair

Tell us a bit about yourself and why you've applied for this traineeship.

Listen for
A short, relevant summary of their course, specialism, one project and why this role. Under 90 seconds. Not a life story.
Probe
"What made you choose your track?"
Report ticks
Answered the question · Right length · Enthusiasm and motivation
Question 2 · Chair

What do you know about Anchorlight Games, and why do you want to work here rather than anywhere else?

Listen for
Has read the advert. Picks out the stylised action games, the mentoring and the live project, and connects them to their own goals.
Probe
"What would you hope to learn in your first year?"
Report ticks
Knew what the role involves · Had researched the studio · Enthusiasm and motivation
Question 3 · Panellist A

Show us one piece of work from your portfolio and talk us through how you made it.

Listen for
Has it ready on screen. Covers their role, the tools, one problem and how they solved it, and what they'd improve. Says "I" for their own part.
Probe
"If you had another week on it, what would you change and why?"
Report ticks
Had work ready to show · Specific evidence · Industry vocabulary · Walked through work clearly
Question 4 · Panellist B

Tell us about a time you worked as part of a team. What did you do, and how did it go?

Listen for
STAR: a named project, their specific contribution, and a result. Honest about any difficulty.
Probe
"What would you do differently in that team now?"
Report ticks
Used STAR · Said "I" for their own part · Honest about skills and gaps
Their questions · Chair

"That's all our questions. Is there anything you'd like to ask us?"

Closing · Chair

"Thanks very much, [name]. If you could wait outside for a few minutes, we'll talk it over and then bring you back in for some quick feedback. You'll also get a written report by email."

Spare questions

Use one only if a candidate finishes with time to spare. The Chair picks. If a candidate's track suits one better, that's fine.

  1. "You've hit a problem in your work that you can't solve. Talk us through what you do."Listen for a method: reproduce, isolate, research, time-box, then ask for help clearly.
  2. "Tell us about a time you got feedback on your work that you didn't agree with at first. What happened?"Calm and reflective: what changed, and the result.
  3. "What's a game you've played recently, and what's one thing you'd change about it?"Specific and critical. Explains why the change helps the player.
  4. "What tools or engines are you most confident in, and which are you still learning?"Honest levels, with something they've made in each.
  5. "Tell us about a time you had a tight deadline."Prioritising and cutting scope, not just staying up all night.
  6. "Where do you see yourself in five years?"Connects to their B1 five-year plan and to this role.
  7. "A teammate isn't doing their share. What do you do?"Talks to them privately first and focuses on the work. Escalates only if needed.
  8. "Why should we offer you the traineeship?"Three reasons matched to the advert, each with proof.

Filling in the report

  1. Open Interview reports Use the link at the top of this page or on the B1 hub, and enter the password interview. Use the same laptop all day, because reports only save on that machine.
  2. Name, and email if you know it Only the name is required. You can add the email later.
  3. Agree each skill together Go through the five groups. Tick Strong if they showed it well, or Work on if they need to improve. Leave both blank if you didn't see it. If you disagree, talk it through briefly; the Chair has the final say.
  4. Overall level Choose from Excellent, Good, Developing or Not yet ready.
  5. Best moment and top target One sentence each. The top target is what you tell them in the feedback, so make it specific: "Finish your examples on the result", not "be more confident".
  6. Build report, then Email to student The email opens with the report already filled in. To send the version with charts, use Print / save PDF and attach it. Then press New student.
  7. At the end of the day Press Download all as spreadsheet to keep a backup of every result.

Giving the two-minute feedback

1 · One strength

Name it and say why it worked. "Your portfolio walkthrough was really clear. You told us what problem you hit and how you fixed it."

2 · One target

Just one, framed as what to do next time. "Next time, finish your team story on the result: what happened because of what you did."

Finish with: "Well done. Your full report will come by email. Write it up in your reflective log today."
