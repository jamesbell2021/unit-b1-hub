01 — What is it

UCAS is a post office

That is genuinely the clearest way to think about it. UCAS does not teach anyone, does not decide who gets in, and does not set entry requirements. It is the service that carries one application to up to five universities so you don't have to write five.

1

application

You fill in one form. Personal details, qualifications, a personal statement, a reference from the college.

5

choices

It goes to up to five courses. They cannot see each other — no university knows where else you applied.

£34.50

one fee

Covers all five. Fee waivers exist if you had free school meals or are care-experienced — check, don't assume.

What UCAS actually does and doesn't do

It does

  • Host the course search for every UK university
  • Carry your application to your five choices
  • Track your offers in one place (the {{hub|UCAS Hub}})
  • Run {{clearing|Clearing}} and {{extra|Extra}} if things don't go to plan
  • Convert qualifications into {{tariff|tariff points}} so courses can compare them

It does not

  • Decide whether you get in — the university does that
  • Set entry requirements — the university does that too
  • Handle every course in the UK. Plenty of courses are applied for directly
  • Give you money. Student finance is a separate organisation and a separate application

02 — Which route

UCAS, or straight to the college?

This is the part that catches people out. Students assume everything goes through UCAS, miss the fact that their chosen course takes direct applications, and either pay a fee they didn't need or miss a deadline that was never on the UCAS calendar.

Proof, from your own course page

Look at the course you are on right now: Level 3 National Extended Diploma in Creative Media Practice (Game Development), course code P00175-AE, Castlereagh, Full Time, Further Education.

That is a Belfast Met course code, not a UCAS one. UCAS course codes look like G4G1 or W610 — short, four characters, letters and numbers. You applied for this course directly on the Belfast Met website. Nobody sent a UCAS form.

The habit that saves you

Every course page on every provider's website has a section called How to apply. Read it. It will either give you a UCAS code or an "Apply now" button that goes to the provider's own form. That single check takes thirty seconds and is the difference between applying and thinking you applied.

03 — How to apply

The six steps

If you decided UCAS is your route, this is the whole process. It takes longer than you think, mostly because of the personal statement and because your reference depends on somebody else.

  1. Register on the UCAS Hub Create an account at ucas.com. You will be asked for a buzzword — that's a code from the college that links your application to Belfast Met so your reference can be attached. Ask for it; don't guess.
  2. Search for courses and pick up to five Use the UCAS course search. For each one, record the entry requirements, the tariff points, the course content and the location. Five is the maximum, not a target — four strong choices beat five lazy ones.
  3. Fill in your details and qualifications Personal details, education history, every qualification including the ones you haven't finished yet. Enter your BTEC exactly as it is titled. Getting the qualification wrong here is one of the most common causes of problems later.
  4. Write your personal statement The part that takes real time. Why this subject, what you have done about it, what you bring. Write it in a separate document and paste it in — do not compose it inside the form. Start early and expect several drafts.
  5. Get your reference Written by the college, not by you. This is why you apply through Belfast Met rather than on your own. Your tutor needs lead time, so the college deadline will be earlier than the UCAS one.
  6. Pay and send £34.50 for up to five choices. Check everything, then submit. It goes to the college first for the reference to be attached, then on to UCAS. Pressing submit is not the end — the college sends it.

The deadline that actually binds you

UCAS's equal consideration deadline for 2027 entry is 13 January 2027 at 18:00. But Belfast Met's internal deadline will be earlier, because staff need time to read your application, check your qualifications and write your reference. Ask what the college date is and work to that one. Nobody has ever been penalised for being early.

The 2027 entry calendar

These are UCAS's published dates for courses starting in autumn 2027 — the cycle that applies if you are finishing this course in summer 2027. Every time is 18:00 UK time.

What "equal consideration" means

Apply by 13 January and the university must consider your application alongside everyone else's. Apply after it and they only have to look at you if places are left. It is not a cut-off, it is a fairness guarantee — and on a competitive course, missing it is close to the same thing.

The thing to notice

Look at the gap between two grades next to each other. One grade can be worth 16 points, and a course asking for 112 will not take 96. That is the entire argument for not coasting in year two — and it is worth writing down in your five-year plan as a specific consequence rather than a vague intention.
