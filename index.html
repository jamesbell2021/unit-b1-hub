01 — Why this unit exists

This is the year it stops being coursework

Last year you learned to make things. This year you find out whether anyone outside this building can tell. That is the entire unit.

2

years

Slap Bang Digital was founded in Belfast in 2024 by one person. In 2026 it was at Gamescom in Cologne with a funded game. Buck Eejit started in 2022 and did the same. Two years is the exact length of your course.

45

seconds

Roughly how long someone spends on a junior applicant's portfolio before deciding. Not because they're lazy — because there are three hundred of them and one afternoon.

The honest version

Nobody in the industry is going to ask what grade you got in B1. What they will ask for is a link. This unit exists because the gap between people who get into games and people who don't is very rarely talent — it's whether there was anything to look at when someone went looking.

You already have a year of work sitting on a hard drive. Games you helped build, art you made, code you wrote, problems you solved at 2am. Right now, none of it is visible to anyone who isn't in this room. Over the next eleven weeks you're going to turn it into a showreel, a virtual gallery, a website and a public repository — plus the boring paperwork that makes people take the interesting stuff seriously.

The unit is graded on evidence, not enthusiasm. "I worked really hard on this" isn't assessable. "Here are three versions of this piece, here's the feedback that caused each change, here's why the final one is better" is.

What you're actually being marked on

Five criteria. All five must be at least a Pass — you cannot trade a brilliant showreel against a missing progression plan.

AC1 · Research

Did you find out how this job really works, from real sources?

AC2 · Problem solving

Did you plan a route, get feedback, and change the plan?

AC3 · Technical skill

Did you actually build the materials, well?

AC4 · Professional practice

Did you behave like someone hireable?

AC5 · Communication

Can you show your work and talk about it?

05 — The local scene

People doing this here

Northern Ireland's games industry is small enough that you can genuinely meet most of it, and that is an advantage, not a limitation. In a city of ten thousand developers you are anonymous. Here, one good conversation gets remembered.

Why small is good news for you

Most of these studios are under fifteen people. That means no keyword filter between you and a human being, hiring that happens through visibility rather than adverts, and founders who were exactly where you are within recent memory. It also means when you turn up to something twice, they know your face.

Studios and companies

Local entrepreneurs worth knowing

Not everyone worth studying makes games. These two built serious companies from Northern Ireland, in industries with nothing to do with games — which is exactly why they're useful: they prove the route (start small here, build something real, stay) works outside games too.

The trap: picking a billionaire you'll never meet

It's the default move — asked for an inspiration, most people reach for Bill Gates, Elon Musk, someone with a Wikipedia infobox and a net worth in the headline. The problem isn't that it's wrong, it's that it's useless as evidence. You can't trace their early route in any detail, you can't contact them, and "I admire Steve Jobs" tells an assessor nothing about you. A local entrepreneur or NI games developer founder is a stronger choice for exactly the opposite reasons: their whole route is traceable, several of them post publicly about how they did it, and some of them are one email or one event away. Pick someone whose journey you can actually learn something concrete from, not just admire from a distance.

Where the money and the doors are

Your task for AC1

Pick three studios or companies from above. Find out what they've made, who works there in the role you want, and what that person did before. Then find one thing they've said publicly — a talk, a devlog, an interview, a post — and quote what you learned from it in your research file. Do the same again with one local entrepreneur, games or otherwise. That is the difference between listing names and researching them — and it's a stronger AC1 submission than a paragraph about a billionaire you found on a rich list.
